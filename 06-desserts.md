# Desserts

Eight desserts that lean on cast iron's even heat to caramelize fruit, crisp edges, and finish a tender crumb. Whole grains, fruit, and moderate sweeteners do the heavy lifting — these aren't health-food versions, just real desserts that don't go overboard.

---

## 1. Skillet Apple Crisp with Oat-Walnut Topping

Cinnamon-tossed apples that blister at the pan edges, capped with a craggy oat-and-walnut topping.

**Time:** 50 min (15 prep · 35 bake) · **Serves:** 8

**Ingredients**
- 6 medium apples (Granny Smith + Honeycrisp), peeled and sliced ¼ inch
- 4 Tbsp butter (filling) + 6 Tbsp cold butter, cubed (topping)
- 3 Tbsp brown sugar (filling) + ⅓ cup brown sugar (topping)
- 1 tsp vanilla
- 1½ tsp cinnamon, divided
- 1 cup rolled oats
- ½ cup whole-wheat flour
- ½ cup walnuts, roughly chopped
- ½ tsp salt

**Method**
1. Heat oven to 350°F.
2. Melt 4 Tbsp butter in a 10-inch skillet over medium-low. Add apples, 3 Tbsp brown sugar, 1 tsp cinnamon, vanilla, and a pinch of salt. Toss; cook 3–4 min until just glossy.
3. In a bowl, mix oats, flour, ⅓ cup brown sugar, walnuts, ½ tsp cinnamon, and salt. Cut in cold butter with fingers until pea-sized clumps form.
4. Scatter topping over apples; do not pack down.
5. Bake 30–35 min until topping is golden and fruit bubbles at the edges.
6. Rest 10 min. Serve warm.

> The pan goes from stovetop sauté to oven without a swap, and retained heat finishes the apples to fork-tender without the topping over-browning.

## 2. Whole-Wheat Skillet Chocolate Chip Cookie

One giant cookie — molten in the middle, crisp at the edge. Half the sweetener of a standard skillet cookie, all of the texture.

**Time:** 30 min (10 prep · 20 bake) · **Serves:** 10–12

**Ingredients**
- ¾ cup butter, softened
- ½ cup brown sugar, packed
- ¼ cup pure maple syrup
- 2 large eggs
- 2 tsp vanilla
- 2 cups whole-wheat flour (or whole-wheat pastry flour)
- 1 tsp baking soda
- ½ tsp salt
- 1¼ cups dark chocolate chips

**Method**
1. Heat oven to 350°F. Lightly butter a 10-inch skillet.
2. Beat butter, brown sugar, and maple syrup until fluffy, ~2 min. Add eggs and vanilla; beat smooth.
3. Stir in flour, baking soda, and salt until just combined. Fold in chocolate chips.
4. Spread dough evenly into the skillet.
5. Bake 18–20 min — the center should look slightly underdone. Don't overbake; carryover heat finishes it.
6. Rest 5 min. Cut into wedges and serve warm.

> Thermal mass keeps the bottom and edges crisping while the center stays gooey — exactly the textural contrast you want in a deep-dish cookie.

## 3. Maple Berry Skillet Crumble

A summery foil to apple crisp. Use whatever berries are in season — frozen berries work without thawing.

**Time:** 45 min (10 prep · 35 bake) · **Serves:** 6–8

**Ingredients**
- 5 cups mixed berries (blueberries, raspberries, halved strawberries; fresh or frozen)
- ⅓ cup maple syrup
- 1 Tbsp lemon juice + 1 tsp zest
- 1 Tbsp + ½ cup whole-wheat flour, divided
- 1 cup rolled oats
- ½ cup raw almonds, roughly chopped
- ¼ cup brown sugar
- ½ tsp cinnamon, pinch salt
- 6 Tbsp butter, melted

**Method**
1. Heat oven to 375°F.
2. Toss berries in a 10-inch skillet with maple syrup, lemon juice, zest, and 1 Tbsp flour.
3. In a bowl, combine oats, ½ cup flour, almonds, brown sugar, cinnamon, and salt. Drizzle in melted butter; stir until clumpy and damp.
4. Scatter topping over berries.
5. Bake 30–35 min until topping is golden and juices bubble thickly at the edges.
6. Cool 15 min — the filling needs time to set.

> The hot pan kick-starts berry juice reduction from below while the topping crisps from above. No soggy bottoms.

## 4. Caramelized Apple Tarte Tatin

Apples caramelize in the skillet's brown-sugar caramel, then a single sheet of pastry goes on top. Flip to reveal glossy fruit. Pears work beautifully if you have them.

**Time:** 45 min (15 prep · 30 bake) · **Serves:** 8

**Ingredients**
- 5–6 small firm-sweet apples — Honeycrisp, Gala, or Pink Lady (or ripe-but-firm Bosc/Anjou pears), halved and cored
- 4 Tbsp butter
- ½ cup brown sugar
- ½ tsp cinnamon, pinch nutmeg
- Pinch salt

**Quick galette dough (pantry version):**
- 1 cup all-purpose flour + ¼ cup whole-wheat flour
- 8 Tbsp cold butter, cubed
- ¼ tsp salt
- 3–4 Tbsp ice water

**Method**
1. Make dough: pulse flours, salt, and butter in a food processor until pea-sized. Add ice water 1 Tbsp at a time until dough just clumps. Pat into a disk, chill 20 min while you start the caramel. Roll out to an 11-inch round on a floured surface.
2. Heat oven to 400°F.
3. Melt butter in a 10-inch skillet over medium. Whisk in brown sugar, cinnamon, nutmeg, and salt; cook 2–3 min until bubbling and dark amber.
4. Off heat, arrange fruit cut-side up, packed snugly.
5. Lay dough over the top; tuck edges down around the fruit with the back of a spoon. Cut 4 small steam vents.
6. Bake 30–35 min until pastry is deep golden. Rest 10 min, then carefully invert onto a plate (do this over the sink — the caramel is hot).

> The skillet is the caramel pan, the baking dish, and the serving cradle. Even heat means no scorch spots in the caramel — make-or-break for tarte tatin.

## 5. German Apple Pancake (Apple Dutch Baby)

A puffy, custardy oven pancake with caramelized apples baked right in.

**Time:** 30 min (10 prep · 20 bake) · **Serves:** 4–6

**Ingredients**
- 2 medium Granny Smith apples, peeled and sliced ¼ inch
- 3 Tbsp butter
- 3 Tbsp brown sugar
- 1 tsp cinnamon
- 4 large eggs
- ⅔ cup whole milk
- ⅔ cup all-purpose flour
- 2 Tbsp maple syrup
- 1 tsp vanilla, pinch salt

**Method**
1. Heat oven to 425°F. Place a 10-inch skillet inside while the oven heats.
2. Blend (or whisk hard) eggs, milk, flour, maple, vanilla, and salt until smooth. Rest 5 min.
3. Pull the hot skillet out. Add butter; swirl. Add apples, brown sugar, and cinnamon; cook on stovetop over medium-high 4–5 min until apples soften and edges brown.
4. Pour batter over the apples. Return to oven.
5. Bake 18–20 min until puffed dramatically and deep gold at the edges. Do not open the oven.
6. Serve immediately — it falls within a few minutes.

> A screaming-hot pan is what makes the batter shoot up the sides into those signature crisp ruffles. A regular pan won't do it.

## 6. Skillet Cocoa Brownies with Olive Oil

Surprisingly dark and fudgy. Olive oil instead of butter, cocoa powder instead of melted chocolate.

**Time:** 35 min (10 prep · 25 bake) · **Serves:** 8–10

**Ingredients**
- ½ cup olive oil
- ⅔ cup brown sugar
- ¼ cup maple syrup
- 2 large eggs
- 1 tsp vanilla
- ¾ cup whole-wheat flour
- ½ cup unsweetened cocoa powder
- ½ tsp baking soda, ½ tsp salt
- ½ cup dark chocolate chips
- Flaky salt to finish

**Method**
1. Heat oven to 350°F. Lightly oil a 10-inch skillet.
2. Whisk olive oil, brown sugar, maple syrup, eggs, and vanilla until glossy and emulsified, ~1 min.
3. Sift cocoa, flour, baking soda, and salt over the wet mixture. Fold until just combined.
4. Stir in ¼ cup of the chocolate chips. Spread in the skillet; scatter remaining chips on top.
5. Bake 20–25 min — start checking at 20 min. A tester should come out with moist crumbs, not wet batter.
6. Cool 10 min. Sprinkle with flaky salt. Cut into wedges.

> Retained heat keeps the bottom dry and chewy while the top stays fudgy — the same dynamic that makes diner brownies so good.

## 7. Olive Oil Yogurt Cake with Roasted Berries

Tender, lemon-bright cake with crackly cast iron edges, served with jammy berries roasted in the same pan first.

**Time:** 45 min (10 prep · 35 bake) · **Serves:** 8

**Ingredients**
- 1½ cups berries (any) + 2 Tbsp maple syrup
- ½ cup olive oil + more for the pan
- ½ cup honey
- ¼ cup brown sugar
- 2 large eggs
- 1 cup plain whole-milk Greek yogurt
- 1 Tbsp lemon zest + 2 Tbsp juice
- 1 tsp vanilla
- 1¼ cups whole-wheat pastry flour (or ¾ cup all-purpose + ½ cup whole-wheat)
- 1 tsp baking powder, ½ tsp baking soda, ½ tsp salt

**Method**
1. Heat oven to 350°F. Toss berries with maple in a 10-inch skillet; roast 8 min until juicy. Scrape into a bowl. Wipe skillet, oil generously.
2. Whisk olive oil, honey, brown sugar, eggs, yogurt, zest, juice, and vanilla until smooth.
3. Whisk in flour, baking powder, baking soda, and salt until just combined — do not overmix.
4. Pour batter into the oiled skillet.
5. Bake 30–35 min until golden, springy in the middle, and a tester comes out clean.
6. Cool 15 min. Spoon roasted berries and their juices over wedges.

> The pan does double duty — fruit roaster, then cake pan. Olive oil + cast iron = a uniquely crisp, lacy crust on the cake's bottom and sides.

## 8. Stovetop Skillet Rice Pudding

The book's only no-bake dessert. Mostly hands-off, six ingredients, eats warm or chilled.

**Time:** 35 min (5 active · 30 simmer) · **Serves:** 4–6

**Ingredients**
- ⅔ cup long-grain white rice
- 4 cups whole milk (or any unsweetened milk)
- ⅓ cup maple syrup (or honey)
- 1 tsp vanilla
- 1 tsp cinnamon
- Pinch salt
- Optional: berries, toasted walnuts to finish

**Method**
1. Combine rice, milk, maple syrup, cinnamon, and salt in a 10-inch cast iron skillet over medium-high. Bring to a low simmer, stirring once.
2. Reduce heat to low so the surface is barely bubbling.
3. Cook uncovered, stirring every 5 min, 25–30 min until rice is tender and the mixture is thick and creamy. It will look loose at 25 min — keep going until a spoon leaves a brief trail.
4. Off heat, stir in vanilla. The pudding tightens as it sits.
5. Serve warm with berries and walnuts, or chill 2 hr+ for a thicker, traditional texture (loosen with a splash of milk if needed).
6. Keeps 4 days in the fridge.

> The skillet's broad surface area evaporates milk faster than a saucepan — pudding is creamy in 30 min instead of 45.
